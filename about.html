Why the fidgets

Hendrik is 14 and lives in Bethesda, Maryland. He prints fidgets for people living with Alzheimer’s, dementia, and similar conditions, and for the people who care for them.

He wanted a simple object for hands that stay busy: something to hold, turn, press, or trace, without instructions to remember or a task to finish. Fidgets made for this kind of care can cost real money. He did not want a shop. He wanted a way to give one.

Charlotte, and the adults

His sister Charlotte helps with outreach. Adults help oversee the project. Hendrik makes the fidgets. Charlotte helps them reach people. The adults stay involved, including in how requests are read.

The Lemonade Society

Hendrik and Charlotte are the mini co-CEOs of The Lemonade Society, a Washington, DC–area book club for children 12 and under inside the LiTEArary Society. Adults supervise the club’s meetings. The club’s habit is to give books where they are needed and where the club can actually get them there, rather than to sell them.

The Fidget Society borrows that habit. The books and the fidgets are different projects. The question is similar: who is this for, and can we reach them?

Starting small

There is one printer, at home in Bethesda. The project is starting small. It is not a clinic, a store, or a large supply program.
