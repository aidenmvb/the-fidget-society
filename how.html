From the note to a follow-up

  1. Tell us who it is for.

    Your name, a way to reach you, the city, and whether this is for one person or a care organization.

  2. We read the request and what we can make.

    An adult helping with the project reads the note and what the printer can take on.

  3. We follow up about a design and a handoff.

    The follow-up is about whether something fits. It does not confirm a date.

A request does not hold a fidget or set a date.

Who may request

A family member or caregiver may ask for one person. A memory-care home or an adult day program may ask for a small group.

Please don’t send medical records, a diagnosis, a date of birth, or proof of income.

A small project

Hendrik prints at home. Requests are read one by one, with the person and the project’s capacity in mind.

An individual request is usually for one fidget. A care organization can explain a small request for more than one person, in general terms. A street address is not needed at this stage.

Close to home

The project is based in Bethesda. A handoff is most realistic nearby, in Maryland, Virginia, and Washington, DC.

A request from farther away is still a request. Availability is part of the follow-up.

Designs and preferences

You can name a design or ask for help choosing. A color is optional. Both depend on the designs and filament on hand.

Not every design will suit every person. If a preference isn’t possible, the follow-up can suggest another motion.
